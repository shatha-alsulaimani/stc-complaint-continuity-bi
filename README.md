# Customer Context Continuity in STC Complaint Handling

**MIS Graduation Project · University of Tabuk · Fall 2026**
Team of 4 · Supervisor: Dr. Asmaa Ahmed

> When a complaint moves between employees, departments or channels, how much of its context is lost, and where?

## Approach
- **Mixed methods:** a survey of 250 people (187 had complained to STC) plus 5 interviews with STC and partner staff, including a telecom engineer, a customer service rep and a complaints supervisor.
- **Theory:** Justice Theory (distributive, procedural, interactional) and Information Processing Theory.
- **Output:** six KPIs, three Power BI dashboards and a recommended decision-support model.

## Key findings
| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Complainants who hit a continuity break | **73.8%** (138 / 187) |
| Had to resubmit information already given | 60.1% |
| Difficulty when transferred between departments | 52.9% |
| Employee unaware of previous actions | 47.8% |
| Complaint status unclear | 45.7% |

The **referral and escalation** stage had the highest difficulty rate. Resolution and closure had the lowest, at 19.6%.

## Dashboards (Power BI)
1. **Complaint Journey Dashboard:** difficulty rate at each stage of the journey
2. **KPI & Customer Experience Dashboard:** the six continuity KPIs
3. **AI-Powered Decision Support Dashboard:** escalation risk and suggested next action

The descriptive → diagnostic → predictive progression is the core idea.

## Recommendation
An AI-powered BI dashboard for employees, with a 360° complaint view, a unified action history and smart routing, and an **mystc** complaint tracker for customers with SLA alerts. Rollout is phased over 17 weeks.
